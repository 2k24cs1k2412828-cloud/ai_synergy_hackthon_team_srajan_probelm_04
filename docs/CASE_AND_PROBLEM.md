# Case Understanding and Problem Investigation

## 1. Case Understanding

The Tourism Track case, *Beyond the Fort*, asks us to investigate a tourism-related problem and formulate a feasible, evidence-supported solution.

Our investigation focuses on lesser-known heritage and cultural destinations across Madhya Pradesh rather than restricting the project to Gwalior alone.

## 2. Leading Problem Hypothesis

Travellers exploring Madhya Pradesh may find it difficult to discover destinations that match their interests, available time, and travel preferences. Relevant information may be spread across multiple digital channels.

*This is a hypothesis informed by desk research and case analysis. It is not yet a measured conclusion about all travellers.*

## 3. Alternative Problem Framings

### A. Destination Discovery Gap
Travellers may struggle to identify relevant lesser-known destinations beyond familiar attractions.

### B. Information Reliability Gap
Travellers may encounter inconsistent, outdated, or difficult-to-verify destination information.

### C. Travel Feasibility Gap
Travellers may have difficulty determining whether a destination fits their available time, transport options, access conditions, and return journey.

## 4. Selection Criteria

We will compare the three framings using:
- Support from the official case material
- Quality of independent external evidence
- Importance to target travellers
- Ability to validate the problem
- Feasibility of building a small prototype
- Consequences of providing incorrect information

## 5. Current Direction

Destination discovery is the leading framing because it provides a focused starting point for a feasible prototype. Information reliability is treated as a necessary design consideration rather than a separate promise that the project has already solved.

The final selection should be revisited if stronger evidence supports a different problem.

## 6. Research Limitations

Our current research basis is desk research, internet sources, and AI-assisted analysis. AI-generated statements are not independent evidence. No primary field research should be claimed unless the team has actually conducted and documented it.

## 7. Evidence Labels

- **CASE EVIDENCE:** A statement explicitly supported by the official case file.
- **EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** A claim supported by an independent source.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A belief that has not been validated.
- **INFERENCE:** An interpretation drawn from evidence.
- **UNKNOWN:** Missing information that could affect the decision.

## 8. What We Still Need to Validate

- How frequently target travellers face destination discovery difficulties
- Which information sources they currently use
- Which factors most influence their destination choices
- Whether the proposed workflow improves discovery compared with ordinary search
- Which destination data can be obtained and maintained reliably
